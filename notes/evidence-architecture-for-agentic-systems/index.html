Day One · Architecture Note

Agentic systems need an evidence architecture.

Agentic systems need more than logs. They need an evidence architecture that connects actions to authority, inputs, evaluation, and approval as the system evolves.

The architectural question

What must the system be able to prove?

What acted, under whose authority, using which evidence and dependencies, with what result, and with what level of assurance?

The key separation

Completion is not quality.

Execution proves that an output exists. Evaluation supplies evidence that the output met defined quality and acceptance criteria.

The constraint

Trace responsibility, not everything.

Preserve the chain of responsibility without archiving every prompt, response, or intermediate message.

Why plan early

Retrofitted logs capture what implementations happen to emit. Evidence architecture begins with what an organization must be able to prove.

The value is controlled evolution.

As agents, services, policies, and automations change independently, the system retains enough memory to distinguish what changed, what acted, what evidence shaped the outcome, and how confidently that outcome was judged.

What remains to prove.

An evidence architecture on paper is not operational proof. The first validation should show one complete workflow preserving identity, authority, evidence lineage, and outcome, while declaring each stage observed, missing, or reconstructed—without collecting unnecessary content.
