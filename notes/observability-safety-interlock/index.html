An AI-Generated Research Summary Passed Its Tests. Is It Ready to Publish?

An AI-generated research summary is being considered for public release. It passes its required output checks, but its production record is incomplete, and one section was created from material that was not authorized for this output.

Is it ready to publish? Passing the checks answers only one of three separate questions.

Three decisions, not one trust score

  1. Did it pass the required output checks? This supports a claim about the tested result.
  2. Can we verify how it was produced? This supports a claim about the production process.
  3. Was it permitted to be produced and published—including each part? This supports the authority to release it.

One answer cannot silently substitute for another. A summary may pass its output checks while its production path remains unverified. It may also pass those checks while containing material that was never permitted for that output.

The consequences differ too. Failed output checks mean the result should not be described as validated. Missing production evidence limits claims about how the result was created. Failed authorization means the prohibited material should not be published.

Permission can differ across parts of an output. Removing one unapproved section does not, by itself, prove that the remainder is safe to release: its surrounding claims, summaries, or conclusions may still depend on restricted material.

What observability contributes

Observability does not make the publication decision. It can preserve the evidence needed to make that decision: which checks ran, how the summary was produced, and which permissions applied.

When required evidence is missing or cannot be verified, the honest response is not to collapse everything into “trusted” or “untrusted.” It is to identify which question can no longer be answered and limit the corresponding claim or action.

A passing test result is therefore not, by itself, a publication decision. It establishes evidence about the tested output; production evidence and authorization must be assessed separately.

The research boundary

This article defines what must remain separate and why. It does not propose a universal stopping policy or a detailed mechanism for continuing, pausing, or recovering when evidence is incomplete. Those implementation questions require their own evidence and will be discussed in a future post.
